notesHTML: '<p>Two white people take on appropriation vs. appreciation and refuse to dodge it. The Hanged Man — Matt’s recurring card, drawn 3 of the 4 times he’s ever cut this deck — presides over the whole hour: suspension between two ways of being, willing surrender rather than giving up. The core move is to reject the clean binary: there’s no filter that spits out yes/no, and anyone selling you one is selling the colonial-purity reflex in spiritual drag. What they build instead is a working framework — <em>participant, not consumer</em>; reciprocity over extraction; find the through-line archetype and its local expression rather than importing the exotic object.</p><p><strong>Listen: </strong><a target="_blank" href="https://open.spotify.com/show/033CfL5f9scqVjOibTK0f5">Spotify</a> · <a target="_blank" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/rewired/id1896945601">Apple Podcasts</a></p><p><strong>In this episode</strong></p><p>* The card: the Neon Tarot, the recurring Hanged Man, and why tarot works at all (78 gateways, the tension of opposites)</p><p>* Why appropriation happens: the colonial wound, and white people severed from their own earth-connection going looking for it elsewhere</p><p>* The “appropriation police” — usually outside the tradition they’re defending — and why gatekeeping often does more harm than good</p><p>* The useful line: private practice vs. setting up as a paid teacher of a tradition you have no relationship to (Matt’s Irish pagan teacher: <em>“you don’t get to teach this yet”</em>)</p><p>* Inspiration, not appropriation: Weston Price, bone broth, turmeric, and finding the local instantiation of a universal archetype</p><p>* Projection and the confession: the rage is about feeling void of culture, not the practice itself</p><p><strong>Lines that landed</strong></p><p>* “A lot of white people are just running around trying to find something they can’t remember.” — Jaye Anne</p><p>* “Me adopting a practice and performing it in private is pretty limited in how much damage it’ll do.
